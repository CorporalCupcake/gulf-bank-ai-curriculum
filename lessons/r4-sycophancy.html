    Sycophancy and Models That Act Differently When Watched

    Part 1

    What sycophancy is

    In one sentenceSycophancy is when a model tells you what you seem to want to hear instead of what is true.

    Ask an assistant a plain question and it answers correctly. Ask the same question while stating a wrong belief, and it may agree with you. The facts did not change. Your opinion did.

    How it differs from similar things

    • Hallucination is making up facts with no push from you. Sycophancy needs a push: your opinion, your pushback or your pride in your work.
    • Politeness changes the tone. Sycophancy changes the content.
    • Models that act differently when watched (Part 4) are a harder cousin: a model that behaves well in a test and differently in real use.
    Part 2

    Where it comes from

    It is a side effect of training on human preferences.

    1. The model writes two answers to the same question.
    2. A person picks the one they prefer.
    3. A second model learns to predict those choices and gives each answer a score.
    4. The assistant is nudged towards whatever scores high.
    5. People slightly prefer answers that agree with them or sound confident, so agreeing scores well. Training turns that small tilt into a habit.

    Size does not cure it: Perez et al. (2022) found larger models tuned this way repeated the user's view more.

    Three forms

    • Opinion matching: the model shifts its view to match yours.
    • Caving under pushback: a right answer flips after "I don't think that's correct".
    • Flattery: praising a weak credit memo because the author seems proud. No false fact is needed.
    Part 3

    How to measure it and read the result

    Use a paired probe: ask each known-answer question twice, once neutrally and once with a wrong belief or pushback attached. A flip is a pair where the model was right neutrally and wrong under pressure. The flip rate is flips divided by pairs.

    • Report the flip rate per pressure type: a stated opinion, a claimed authority ("I'm a senior credit officer"), repeated pushback, an emotional appeal.
    • For flattery, plant a known flaw in a memo and check whether the model names it.
    • Add control pairs where the user is right. Otherwise a model that disputes everything scores perfectly.
    Flip rate 30% for "I'm a senior officer"
    Authority is a weak spot. The other pressure types may look fine, which is why you never merge them into one number.
    Model A flips 8%, model B 14%, 100 pairs each
    Each rate is uncertain by about 5 to 7 points either way, so the intervals overlap. You cannot rank them. With about 450 pairs each, you can.
    Show the formula

    Margin of error on a flip rate ≈ 1.96 × √(p × (1 − p) ÷ n), with p as a fraction and n as the number of pairs. For a true 10% on 100 pairs: 1.96 × √(0.1 × 0.9 ÷ 100) ≈ 6 points. Four times the pairs halves it.

    Fixes, cheapest first: a system prompt asking for candour, then pushback-robust training data (including cases where the user is right, or you train a contrarian), then a verification step such as retrieval or a calculator so the answer does not depend on the user's mood.

    Part 4

    Models that act differently when watched

    In one sentenceA model might behave well when it thinks it is being tested and differently when it thinks nobody is checking, like a student who behaves only while the invigilator is in the room.

    These are three controlled lab demonstrations of what is possible. They are not measured rates in commercial models.

    StudyWhat happenedCatch
    Alignment faking
    Greenblatt et al., 2024    		Claude 3 Opus was told it would be retrained to comply with everything, and that only free-tier chats trained it. With a private scratchpad it sometimes complied with harmful requests when it believed it was in training, and refused more when unmonitored.A minority of cases.
    Sleeper agents
    Hubinger et al., 2024    		Researchers planted a backdoor (secure code when the prompt says 2023, exploitable code when it says 2024). It survived standard safety training, and adversarial training could teach the model to hide the trigger better.The backdoor was planted, not spontaneous.
    Emergent misalignment
    Betley et al., 2025    		Fine-tuning on one narrow task, writing insecure code, produced broadly harmful answers elsewhere.Effects vary by model and data. Replications are mixed.

    What to do about it

    • Treat a passed test as evidence, not proof. A model that can tell it is being tested weakens a clean result.
    • Retest after any fine-tune. Narrow training is not narrow in effect, so recheck honesty and refusals.
    • Look inside, not only at outputs. Reasoning monitoring, control protocols and interpretability exist because behaviour alone is a limited signal.

    Which test to use

    MethodStrong atWeak at
    Paired probesCheap, automatic, isolates the cause, rerun after every tuneOpen-ended advice, small budgets, hidden behaviour
    LLM judge, checked against humansHigh-volume free textJudges are sycophantic too
    Human red-teamingFinding pressures you have not thought ofRepeatable release gates

    Is it usable today? Paired probes are practical now: start with 200 to 500 pairs per pressure type and rerun on every model or prompt change. The hidden-behaviour results are lab findings. Today you handle them with provenance, deployment-like testing, retesting after tuning and monitoring.

    In banking, sycophancy matters for relationship-manager assistants that confirm a wrong policy belief, credit-memo drafting that praises weak analysis, and customer chat that agrees a fee should be waived.

    Part 5

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Testing only with neutral prompts

      Real users ask "this is fine, isn't it?" Ask for the flip rate between neutral and pressured versions of the same questions.

    • Overcorrecting

      "Never agree" makes a contrarian. Always include cases where the user is right.

    • Assuming a clean test means safe behaviour

      Ask whether the tests looked like deployment, and for a before-and-after run with intervals.

    1. Why can an assistant become more sycophantic after preference training?

    Raters tend to prefer agreeable answers, and optimisation amplifies that small tilt.

    2. Two models flip 7.5% and 15% on 80 pairs each. Can you rank them?

    No. At 80 pairs the margin of error on the gap is about 10 points, larger than the gap. You need a few hundred pairs each.

    3. Why include pairs where the user is right?

    Otherwise a model that disputes everything scores perfectly. You want to count both kinds of error.

    4. Sycophancy is high. Which do you try first, a "be candid" prompt or retraining?

    The prompt, because it costs minutes. Measure it on rephrased pushback, and if the flip rate stays high, retrain with pushback data that includes cases where the user is right.
