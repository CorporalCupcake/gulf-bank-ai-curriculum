    AI Frameworks: EU AI Act, ISO 42001 and NIST

    Part 1

    What the three frameworks are

    In one sentenceThe EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF are three different kinds of thing: a law with fines, a standard you can be audited against, and a voluntary playbook.

    None of them is a Gulf law. They reach a Gulf bank through European customers, vendors and counterparties. You need a map of them, not a legal opinion.

    How they differ

    EU AI ActISO/IEC 42001NIST AI RMF
    What it isEU regulation, risk-tiered, with penaltiesCertifiable standard for an AI management system (plan, do, check, improve)US voluntary framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage
    Who it reachesAnyone whose AI or its output is used in the EU, via branches, customers or vendorsNobody, unless a customer, regulator or the bank adopts itNobody; US supervisors reference it
    Part 2

    How each one works

    The EU AI Act: traffic lights on use cases

    Take a bank with a credit scorecard, a fraud detector and a customer chatbot. The Act sorts them by what they are used for, not by the technology.

    1. Banned. A short list, such as social scoring and emotion recognition at work.
    2. High-risk. Uses listed in an annex. The headline bank case is credit scoring of natural persons. Fraud detection is expressly excluded.
    3. Light transparency duties. For chatbots and synthetic media: people must be told they are dealing with AI.
    4. Everything else. No special duties.

    So the personal-loan scorecard is high-risk, the fraud detector is not, and the chatbot needs a disclosure. SME loan scoring is not caught by the credit entry, but confirm with counsel. A 2026 "Digital Omnibus" defers high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027, so check the Official Journal before quoting a date.

    ISO/IEC 42001: are you certified?

    It describes an AI management system: the policies, roles and routines an organisation runs around its AI. An outside auditor can certify you against it. A European counterparty may ask whether you hold it.

    NIST AI RMF: structure for a risk conversation

    RMF stands for Risk Management Framework. Its four functions are questions: Govern (who is accountable?), Map (what is the context?), Measure (what do the tests show?) and Manage (how do we monitor and respond?).

    Part 3

    How to use them together

    They do not conflict. Do not run three parallel projects.

    1. Anchor on your own regulator's expectations and model risk policy (see the Gulf regulators lesson).
    2. Keep one internal set of controls.
    3. Keep a table mapping each control to each framework.
    4. Answer each outside request from that table: a European customer's questionnaire, an ISO audit, a US counterparty.
    How to think about itAsk "which kind of thing is this?" first. A law needs a legal reading and a date. A standard needs evidence for an audit. A guide only needs you to borrow its structure.
    If you need to...Reach for
    Know if a use case is regulated in the EUEU AI Act, with counsel
    Prove good practice to a counterpartyISO/IEC 42001 certification
    Structure an internal risk reviewNIST AI RMF
    Satisfy your Gulf supervisorIts own expectations, not any of these
    • Strong at: the Act gives clear tiers and real penalties; ISO gives audit evidence; NIST gives a common vocabulary.
    • Weak at: the Act's dates keep moving; ISO certifies the process, not each model; NIST has no enforcement.

    In banking, the one to check first is the Act's credit scoring entry, since it can reach personal-loan models used for EU customers. Treat every tiering as a first screen for counsel, not a ruling.

    Part 4

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Mixing up their kinds

      Saying a bank "must comply with NIST" or that ISO 42001 is a law. Only the Act binds, and only where it reaches you.

    • Classifying by technology instead of use

      "It's generative AI, so high-risk" or "it's just a chatbot, so nothing". Ask what decision the output feeds and who is affected.

    • Paper compliance

      A policy and a certificate, but no tests run and no records kept. Ask for the last test run and incident record.

    • Running three projects

      Separate efforts for each framework duplicate work. Keep one control set and a mapping table.

    1. What kind of thing is each: EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF?

    The Act is binding law with penalties, scaled by use-case risk. ISO/IEC 42001 is a certifiable management-system standard. NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework.

    2. Which is high-risk under the Act's credit entry: personal-loan scoring, card-fraud detection or SME loan scoring?

    Personal-loan scoring. The entry covers natural persons' creditworthiness. Fraud detection is excluded and SME scoring is not caught. Confirm with counsel.

    3. A European counterparty asks if you are certified. Which framework is that?

    ISO/IEC 42001, the only one of the three you can be certified against.

    4. What are NIST AI RMF's four functions?

    Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.

    5. How do you avoid triple work across the frameworks?

    Keep one internal control set anchored on your regulator and model risk policy, with a table mapping each control to each framework.
