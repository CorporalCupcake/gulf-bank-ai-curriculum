    Conformal Prediction and Causal ML

    Part 1

    Two different questions

    In one sentenceConformal prediction puts an honest error bar around any model's guess, and causal ML measures what your action changed, which is a different number from the guess.

    Running example: a café that forecasts daily sales and sends vouchers by text.

    • Conformal question: "The model says 500 cups tomorrow. How wrong could that be?" You want a range where "90%" really means nine days in ten.
    • Causal question: "Who buys because of the voucher?" A score that says who will buy cannot tell you, since many would have bought anyway.
    Part 2

    Conformal prediction: ranges you can trust

    Think of a tailor who checks how far off past fittings were, then pads tomorrow's estimate by the typical miss. That is the whole idea. It works around any model: a neural net, boosted trees or a vendor black box.

    How it works

    1. Train any model for daily sales.
    2. Hold back some days it has never seen (the calibration set), for example 500 days.
    3. For each, record the miss: the gap between forecast and actual.
    4. Sort the misses and pick the one at the 90th percentile, nudged up slightly to allow for the new day itself. Say it is 60 cups.
    5. Every new forecast becomes forecast ± 60: "500, range 440 to 560".

    How to read it

    A 90% range means about 9 in 10 future actuals land inside. The only assumption is that new days look like the calibration days (nobody could tell them apart if you shuffled them together). It assumes nothing about the model or the shape of the errors.

    • Wide range: the model is weak. The range is still valid, just not useful. Conformal cannot fix a poor model.
    • Average, not every case: 90% holds across all days, not for each kind of day. Busy Fridays may be under-covered and quiet Tuesdays over-covered.
    • Fix for that: divide each miss by an estimate of how hard the day is, so ranges widen on hard days. The guarantee still holds if that estimate is rough.
    • Classification: it returns a set of labels. A clear case gets one label, a confusing one gets several, which is a natural signal to send it to a human.

    When the data shift

    If the future stops resembling the calibration days (Ramadan trade, a new product, adapting fraudsters), coverage quietly slips. Two repairs exist.

    • Only the mix changed (different customers, same behaviour): reweight calibration cases by how much more likely they are in the new mix.
    • Steady drift over time: adaptive conformal inference watches recent misses and widens or narrows the range as it goes.
    • Behaviour itself changed: neither helps. You need fresh labelled data.

    Always track real coverage on recent cases. A persistent gap against 90% is your alarm.

    Part 3

    Causal ML: who responds to the action

    Say the café sent vouchers mostly to its regulars. Regulars spend more anyway, so comparing voucher and no-voucher customers credits the voucher with spend that was coming regardless. This is confounding, like judging a gym by how fit its members are.

    A randomised test is the clean fix. With only past data, you adjust for what you know about each customer. Double machine learning (double ML) does this with flexible models.

    How it works

    1. Predict spend from customer features (visits, past spend).
    2. Predict who gets a voucher from the same features.
    3. Keep the leftovers: actual spend minus predicted spend, and voucher (yes/no) minus predicted chance.
    4. Compare the leftovers. The slope of leftover spend against leftover voucher is the voucher's effect.

    How to read it

    • An effect of 45 means "a voucher adds about AED 45 per customer". It does not mean "voucher customers spend 45 more", which is the confounded comparison.
    • Why "double": two prediction models, any ML method. The final step barely cares about their small errors.
    • Cross-fitting: each customer is scored by models that never saw them, so overfitting cannot soak up the signal.
    • Who responds: let the effect depend on features. Meta-learners build effect estimators from ordinary models. Causal forests are random forests that split customers by different effect, not different spend.
    • Who to call: often mid-range customers, not the already-loyal or the hopeless. A purchase score alone would pick the loyal.
    Part 4

    Which to use

    ToolAnswersNeeds
    Plain prediction intervalRange, if the model's error shape is rightNormal-looking errors
    ConformalRange with a real 90% hit rateHeld-out calibration data, no drift
    Naive comparisonTreated minus untreatedRandom assignment
    Double MLEffect of an action from historyAll confounders measured, overlap
    Randomised testEffect, cleanlyTime and the ability to experiment
    • Conformal is strong at: wrapping any model, needing few assumptions, and costing one pass over held-out data.
    • Conformal is weak at: drifting data, per-segment guarantees, tiny calibration sets (a 90% range wants a few hundred cases) and extreme levels such as 99.9%.
    • Causal ML is strong at: separating effect from selection and finding who responds.
    • Causal ML is weak at: hidden confounders (such as a manager's hunch) and customers who appear in only one group, where it extrapolates silently.
    Show the formulas

    Conformal range = prediction ± q, where q is the miss at rank ⌈(n + 1) × 0.9⌉ among n sorted calibration misses. The "+ 1" is the nudge for the new case. A new case's miss is equally likely to rank anywhere among them, so it lands in the worst 10% only about 10% of the time.

    In banking, conformal ranges suit treasury cash-flow forecasts and flagging confusing complaints for review, and causal ML suits deciding which customers a campaign really moves. Validate any uplift claim with a hold-out experiment.

    Part 5

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Calibrating on training data

      Misses on data the model has seen are too small, so the range looks tighter than reality. Use separate held-out cases.

    • Expecting 90% in every segment

      The guarantee is on average. Check coverage by segment, or use difficulty-aware scores.

    • Trusting a score as an effect

      "Likely to buy" is not "buys because of the offer". Only a causal method or a test answers the second.

    • Assuming double ML fixes everything

      It cannot adjust for what you did not measure. Unmeasured confounders still bias it.

    1. A forecast is 500 and the 90% conformal miss is 60. What is the range?

    440 to 560.

    2. Your conformal range is very wide. What does that say?

    The model is weak. The range is still valid, but you need a better model to narrow it.

    3. Customer mix changes after a new product launch. What can you do?

    Reweight the calibration cases toward the new mix, and watch real coverage on recent cases.

    4. Voucher customers spend AED 80 more than others, but double ML says 45. Why the gap?

    Vouchers went to bigger spenders, so the naive gap includes spend that was coming anyway. Double ML removes it.

    5. What does cross-fitting do?

    Each customer is scored by models that never saw them, so overfitting cannot absorb the signal.
