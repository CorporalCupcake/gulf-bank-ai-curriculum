    Paying for Thought: Reasoning Models, Test-Time Compute and Models You Run Yourself

    What a "thinking" model buys you and costs you, when voting beats thinking, and how small and open-weight models let a bank run AI inside its own walls.

    Contents

    The whole lesson in one sentenceA reasoning model spends extra tokens to be right more often on hard problems, with fading returns and a growing bill, while a small open-weight model trades some capability for control; the skill is knowing which problems deserve which.

    Part 1

    The intuition

    "What is 17 × 24?" gets an answer in a second. "Does this trade finance file breach our sanctions policy?" needs an analyst who reads, cross-checks and backtracks. Same person, different effort.

    A reasoning model does the same: it writes a long working-out first, tries approaches, checks itself, then answers. The extra tokens are scratch paper: better answers on hard problems, at the price of money and waiting time. Spending compute at answer time rather than training time is called test-time compute.

    The second half covers running a smaller model yourself, for when data cannot leave the bank: distillation (a small model imitates a big one), quantization (numbers stored in fewer bits) and open weights (published parameters you can host).

    Hold onto thisThinking is a dial, not a feature. Give each use case the lowest setting that meets its accuracy target: cost keeps growing, accuracy flattens. Model size follows the same rule: the smallest model that clears the bar.
    Part 2

    How it really works

    A5.1 · What thinking buys you, and what it costs

    How it learns to think. Reinforcement learning (see M1) rewards right final answers on checkable problems: maths, code, logic. Habits like "wait, that doesn't add up, retry" emerge because they win rewards.

    What it buys. Like a student given more exam time. On checkable tasks (multi-step arithmetic, code, reconciling figures, rules with exceptions) accuracy rises with thinking, but each doubling of tokens adds less. On recall or style tasks, such as summarising an email, there is little to gain.

    What it costs.

    • Money. Thinking tokens are output tokens, the dearer kind, and billed even if you only see a summary.
    • Latency. A 20,000-token think at 80 tokens a second is a four-minute wait. That rules it out for card authorisations and live chat.
    • Variance. Thinking length differs per question. Budget with a distribution, not an average.

    This curve is what vendors sell as an effort or thinking budget setting. Models ration effort badly: they overthink easy questions and can talk themselves out of a correct answer.

    The other way to spend test-time compute is voting. Like polling five analysts and taking the majority view, self-consistency asks the same question several times and takes the most common answer. Independent errors scatter while the right answer repeats. The limit: if the model misreads a clause the same way every time, ten votes are ten copies of one mistake. If the model is right less than half the time, voting makes things worse.

    Verifiers. Voting asks "which answer is most common?". A verifier asks "which is correct?", and wins when checking is easier than solving, like running unit tests or recomputing a total. Some verifiers score each reasoning step (R3).

    Is the visible reasoning true? Not necessarily. It can omit a hint the model used or rationalise afterwards. Treat it as evidence, not an audit trail (R4); this matters for explainability claims to validators.

    A5.2 · Small and on-premises models

    Two techniques shrink a model; pruning, which drops weights that add little, is a third.

    TechniqueWhat it doesWhat you give up
    DistillationA small "student" trains on a large "teacher's" outputs. Like an apprentice learning from a master.Breadth: good on taught tasks, weaker elsewhere. Some providers forbid training on their outputs.
    QuantizationStores each weight in fewer bits (16 to 8 or 4). Like a lower-resolution photo: same picture, a quarter to a half of the memory.A little precision. Tiny loss at 8 bits, small at 4 bits for large models, growing fast below (R12).

    Details are in P3. Two things fill GPU memory.

    • Weights. Parameters × bytes each. 70 billion at 16 bits is 140 GB; at 4 bits, 35 GB.
    • KV cache. Each user's working memory of the text so far (M1), like a desk that fills with open files. It grows with context length and users, and can exceed the weights.

    So size on-premises services for concurrency and document length, not just model size (Simulation 3).

    Open weights are not open source. You can run the published parameters, but training data and code are often withheld. Licences vary: some add user thresholds, revenue clauses or bans on training other models with outputs. Legal and model-risk teams must read the licence, because you now own a component you must validate (G3).

    Why a bank runs models itself, most decisive first:

    • Data that cannot leave a jurisdiction or controlled environment (P4).
    • Predictable cost at high volume.
    • Control of latency and availability.

    The price is engineering: serving, scaling, patching, monitoring, GPU supply. A sovereign cloud is a middle path (A11).

    Part 3

    How it fails, and how to spot it

    FailureWhat it looks likeHow to spot it
    OverthinkingLong thinking on a trivial request; a right first answer dropped for a wrong one.Compare accuracy at low and high effort on your own set. If high is no better, stop paying.
    Cost or latency surpriseBill is several times forecast, or a live journey times out: thinking tokens were not counted.Track all output tokens at the gateway; test the 95th percentile, not the mean.
    Unfaithful explanationsVisible reasoning is quoted to a regulator as the rationale, but it was a plausible story.Change an input and check the decision moves as the explanation claims.
    Correlated errors under votingTen samples agree, confidently, and are wrong.Measure the consistently-wrong share on labelled data. Agreement is not accuracy.
    Quantization regressions4-bit matches on English benchmarks but breaks on Arabic, long documents or numbers.Evaluate per language and task, original against quantized (A4).
    Ignoring KV cacheThe model "fits", then runs out of memory at peak.Size for peak users and longest documents; load-test.
    Licence and provenance gapsOpen model deployed without checking restrictions; version drifts unnoticed.Keep a register: checkpoint hash, licence, source, date, validation result.
    Part 4

    Self-check

    1. What is test-time compute, and name two ways to spend it.

    Compute spent when the model answers, not when it was trained. Two ways: thinking at length first, or sampling several answers and voting or verifying.

    2. A model writes 12,000 thinking tokens per answer. Where is that fine, and where is it a problem?

    At 80 tokens a second that is a wait of 150 seconds, and 5,000 answers cost about AED 3,300 in thinking alone. Fine for an overnight batch of covenant checks. A problem for live chat or card authorisation, where seconds matter and volume multiplies cost.

    3. A single sample is right 70% of the time. What does voting over several samples do, and what limits it?

    It sharpens what the model already leans towards: three votes lift 70% to about 78%, at three times the cost. It assumes errors are scattered. Where the model is consistently wrong, voting adds nothing.

    4. Why is voting useless if the model's wrong answers are all the same wrong answer?

    Voting needs scattered errors and a repeating right answer. If every sample makes the same mistake, the majority is that mistake.

    5. Why can a quantized 32B model still need several GPUs?

    Quantizing shrinks the weights (32 GB at 8 bits) but not the KV cache. With 50 users at 8,000 tokens each the cache is around 100 GB, over three times the weights. Size for users and document length, not just the model.

    6. What is the difference between distillation and quantization?

    Distillation trains a different, smaller model to imitate a larger one. Quantization keeps the same model and stores its numbers in fewer bits.

    7. List three checks before approving an open-weight model for production.

    Licence terms for commercial use; provenance and version record; evaluation on our own tasks and languages against the current model.

    8. Why might a reasoning model make a customer chatbot worse even if it is more accurate?

    Latency and cost. A long wait loses the customer, and thinking multiplies cost at chat volume. Use a fast model with retrieval and escalate hard cases.
