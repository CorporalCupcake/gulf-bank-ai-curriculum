    A12.1 · A12.2 Day one ★ About 30 minutes

    Who Expects What: CBUAE, SAMA, SDAIA and the Data Protection Laws

    A working map of the rules and expectations that touch an AI system at a UAE or Saudi bank: what is hard law, what is supervisory expectation, what is principle, and which questions the compliance team will ask you before launch.

    Contents

    The whole lesson in one sentenceThere is no single "AI law" for a Gulf bank; there are three layers (the central bank's supervisory expectations, national data protection law, and national AI principles), and the data scientist's job is to know which layer a given system triggers and to leave evidence for each.

    Part 1

    The intuition

    Say your model declines some applications and a customer complains. Three people ask three different questions: the supervisor asks who was accountable and whether the customer was treated fairly, the data protection authority asks what personal data went in and why, and the national AI body asks whether published principles were respected.

    Think of a restaurant: the health inspector can close it, the tax office can fine it, and a food guide only gives opinions. Three layers, strongest to softest:

    • Hard law. Statutes with penalties: the UAE and Saudi data protection laws, and DIFC Regulation 10.
    • Supervisory expectation. What the central bank (CBUAE or SAMA) expects of banks it licenses. Often not a statute, but treat it as binding in practice.
    • Principles and strategy. Ethics principles such as SDAIA's. They shape what regulators call reasonable. Do not call them "the law".
    Hold onto thisAsk two questions of every system: where does it sit and what does it touch (personal data, a customer decision, a vendor, data leaving the country). The answers, not the label "AI", decide which rules apply.
    Part 2

    How it really works

    Central bank and national AI expectations (A12.1)

    UAE. The CBUAE supervises onshore banks. Its February 2026 guidance note on responsible AI/ML reportedly puts accountability on the board and senior management, expects documented governance and human oversight, and says discriminatory or manipulative systems should not be deployed. It builds on the Model Management Standards for inventorying, validating and monitoring any model (G3: what counts as a model). This comes from secondary summaries, so read the original before quoting it. It appears to cover onshore firms, not DIFC or ADGM; unconfirmed.

    Saudi Arabia. SAMA's cyber security and third-party rules matter to any AI build. A dedicated SAMA AI document could not be confirmed, so ask Compliance. SDAIA is the national AI body and administers the data protection law. Its AI Ethics Principles (fairness, privacy, accountability, transparency, human benefit) are not binding alone.

    What the supervisors share matters more than how they differ. They want:

    • Named accountability. A senior owner, not "the model", and an inventory of models.
    • Independent challenge. Validation by people who did not build it (G3: validation).
    • Fair, explainable treatment. Evidence you tested for discrimination (G2: fairness) and can explain a decision.
    • Human oversight where stakes are high. Someone can override and stop the system.
    • Control of third parties. The bank still owns the outcome, like a lost parcel is still yours when a courier carries it.

    Data protection laws, and DIFC Regulation 10 (A12.2)

    • UAE onshore. The federal PDPL (Decree-Law 45 of 2021) covers lawful basis, notices, subject rights, security and cross-border transfers, and reportedly a right to object to solely automated decisions. Whether banking data is carved out is a question for the Data Protection Officer.
    • DIFC. Data Protection Law (Law No. 5 of 2020) plus Regulation 10 on personal data processed by autonomous systems, the one regional rule written for AI-type processing. The deployer carries the main duty, users must be told, and high-risk use needs certification. Enforcement dates and amendments are unconfirmed.
    • ADGM. Its own regulations; the same questions apply.
    • Saudi Arabia. The PDPL, enforceable since September 2024 with SDAIA as authority, covers lawful basis, rights, limits on transfers abroad, and penalties up to imprisonment for some offences.

    In practice, be able to state five facts about any system:

    • Which personal data fields go in, and why (G2: privacy basics).
    • The legal basis. Legal decides, based on what you do with the data.
    • Where data is processed and stored, including in a vendor LLM (A11: sovereign AI).
    • Whether it drives a decision about a person, and what the customer can ask for.
    • How long data and logs are kept, and how deletion reaches training sets and logs.

    Turning it into build practice

    Turn each legal sentence into something a reviewer can inspect. The usual tool is a one-page AI use-case record, filled in before build: purpose, owner, data fields, legal basis, vendors and regions, human intervention, tests and monitoring. Like a pre-flight checklist: boring, and it catches things.

    Part 3

    How it fails, and how to spot it

    • Quoting a principle as law, or the reverse

      "SDAIA requires X" about principles; or dismissing a supervisory note because it says "guidance".

      Spot itAsk which layer it comes from and read the original text.
    • Treating a vendor as a shield

      "The cloud provider is compliant, so we are." The bank stays accountable for outcomes and data location.

      Spot itCan you name where prompts and customer data are processed and stored, and under which contract terms?
    • Personal data hiding in prompts, logs and "anonymised" sets

      Staff paste customer details into a tool and logs keep them. Or names are removed but date of birth, postcode and transaction patterns still identify people.

      Spot itSample the logs for names and ID numbers, and try to re-identify a few records.
    • Assuming one jurisdiction covers all

      A DIFC rule applied to an onshore bank, or Saudi transfer limits forgotten in a regional model.

      Spot itList each legal entity and each country whose customers' data the system touches. Check each separately.
    • Human oversight in name only

      A reviewer clicks approve on hundreds of AI decisions an hour.

      Spot itLook at override rates and time per review. Near-zero overrides is a warning, not a success.
    • Building first, asking Legal last

      The proof of concept becomes a pilot with real data before anyone agrees a legal basis.

      Spot itIs there a use-case record with Legal sign-off from before real data was used?
    Part 4

    Self-check

    1. Name the three layers and give one example of each for a Gulf bank.

    Hard law: UAE or Saudi data protection law, DIFC Regulation 10. Supervisory expectation: CBUAE model management standards or SAMA's frameworks. Principles: SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles.

    2. A DIFC bank is building a loan-pricing model. Which more likely binds it directly, the CBUAE note or DIFC Regulation 10, and why the hedge?

    Regulation 10, if personal data is processed by an autonomous system. The CBUAE note is reported as aimed at onshore firms, and the DFSA supervises DIFC firms. The scope is unconfirmed, so say "likely" and let Legal confirm.

    3. What five design facts should you be able to state about any system touching personal data?

    Which fields and why; the legal basis; where data is processed and stored; whether it drives a decision about a person and what the customer can ask for; retention, including how deletion reaches training sets and logs.

    4. Your vendor says its LLM service is "fully compliant". What do you ask?

    Compliant with what, where, under which contract? Where do prompts go, how long are they kept, are they used for training? The bank stays accountable, so you need the facts yourself.

    5. Why is "human in the loop" sometimes a weak control?

    Reviewers short of time, information or authority approve almost everything, so the loop only adds delay. Measure override rate and time per case.

    6. A colleague says "SAMA requires banks to do X with AI". What do you do before repeating it?

    Ask for the document and clause. No dedicated SAMA AI instrument was confirmed, so the claim may come from the general frameworks, SDAIA, or a blog. Verify with Compliance.
