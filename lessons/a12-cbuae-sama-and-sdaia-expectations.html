    P6.8 Day one ★ Priority About 10 minutes

    Gulf Regulators: CBUAE, SAMA and SDAIA

    Part 1

    Who expects what

    In one sentenceIn the Gulf, the rules on bank AI come in three layers: hard law with penalties, what your central bank expects, and softer national principles. Know which layer a statement comes from before you repeat it.

    Say your loan model declines a customer, and the customer complains. Three different people ask three different questions. The supervisor asks who was accountable and whether the customer was treated fairly. The data protection authority asks what personal data went in and why. The national AI body asks whether its published principles were respected.

    Think of a restaurant. The health inspector can close it, the tax office can fine it, and a food guide only gives opinions.

    What each layer means in practice

    • Hard law. Statutes with penalties: the UAE and Saudi data protection laws, and DIFC Regulation 10.
    • Supervisory expectation. Often not a statute, but treat it as binding in practice, because the supervisor inspects you against it.
    • Principles. They shape what regulators call "reasonable". Do not call them "the law".

    Who is who

    • CBUAE is the Central Bank of the UAE. It supervises onshore UAE banks.
    • SAMA is the Saudi Central Bank.
    • SDAIA is Saudi Arabia's national data and AI authority. It publishes AI ethics principles and administers the Saudi data protection law.
    • DIFC and ADGM are financial free zones in Dubai and Abu Dhabi with their own rules.
    Part 2

    What supervisors want from your AI

    Their wording differs, but the asks are similar. Walk through them with the loan model as the example.

    1. Named accountability. A senior owner for the loan model, not "the model", plus an inventory of all models.
    2. Independent challenge. People who did not build it validate it.
    3. Fair, explainable treatment. Evidence that you tested for discrimination, and that you can explain a decline.
    4. Human oversight where stakes are high. Someone can override and stop the system.
    5. Control of third parties. If a vendor model scores the loans, the bank still owns the outcome, like a lost parcel is still yours when a courier carries it.

    What we know about the UAE and Saudi Arabia

    • CBUAE. A February 2026 guidance note on responsible AI and machine learning reportedly puts accountability on the board and senior management, and expects documented governance and human oversight. It builds on the CBUAE Model Management Standards. This comes from secondary summaries, so read the original before quoting it. It appears to cover onshore firms, not DIFC or ADGM; that is unconfirmed.
    • SAMA. Its cyber security and third-party rules matter to any AI build. A dedicated SAMA AI document could not be confirmed, so ask Compliance.
    • SDAIA. Its AI Ethics Principles (fairness, privacy, accountability, transparency, human benefit) are not binding on their own.
    Part 3

    Data protection laws

    In one sentenceData protection laws are the hard law in this picture: they decide what personal data you may use, on what basis, and where it may go.
    PlaceLawWorth knowing
    UAE onshoreFederal PDPL (Decree-Law 45 of 2021)Lawful basis, notices, rights, security, cross-border transfers. Reportedly a right to object to solely automated decisions. Ask the Data Protection Officer whether banking data is carved out.
    DIFCData Protection Law (Law No. 5 of 2020) and Regulation 10Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by autonomous systems: the one regional rule written for AI-type processing. The deployer carries the main duty and users must be told.
    ADGMIts own regulationsSame questions apply; check the text.
    Saudi ArabiaPDPL, with SDAIA as authorityEnforceable since September 2024. Covers lawful basis, rights, limits on transfers abroad, and penalties up to imprisonment for some offences.

    Enforcement dates and amendments for the DIFC rules are unconfirmed, so check them with Legal.

    Five facts to state about any system

    • Which personal data fields go in, and why.
    • The legal basis. Legal decides, based on what you do with the data.
    • Where data is processed and stored, including inside a vendor LLM.
    • Whether it drives a decision about a person, and what the customer can ask for.
    • How long data and logs are kept, and how deletion reaches training sets and logs.

    The usual tool is a one-page AI use-case record, filled in before build: purpose, owner, data fields, legal basis, vendors and regions, human intervention, tests and monitoring. Like a pre-flight checklist: boring, and it catches things.

    Part 4

    Which question applies to my system?

    Ask two things of every system: where does it sit (onshore, DIFC, ADGM, Saudi Arabia) and what does it touch (personal data, a customer decision, a vendor, data leaving the country). The answers decide which rules apply, not the label "AI".

    If your system...Then think about
    Uses customer personal dataThe data protection law of each place it touches. Legal basis, notices, rights.
    Makes or drives decisions about peopleFairness testing, explanations, human override, any right to object.
    Uses a vendor modelWhere prompts and data are processed and stored, and the contract terms. The bank stays accountable.
    Sits in DIFCRegulation 10 more likely than the CBUAE note. Confirm with Legal.
    • Strong at: thinking in layers keeps you from over- or under-reacting to any one document.
    • Weak at: this is a teaching summary, not legal advice. It cannot capture exemptions or recent amendments.

    In banking, every one of these rules ends up as something a reviewer can inspect: an owner, an inventory entry, a use-case record and test evidence. Confirm real cases with Legal and Compliance.

    Part 5

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Quoting a principle as law, or the reverse

      Saying "SDAIA requires X" about principles, or dismissing a supervisory note because it says "guidance". Ask which layer it comes from and read the original.

    • Treating a vendor as a shield

      "The cloud provider is compliant, so we are." The bank stays accountable for outcomes and data location.

    • Personal data hiding in prompts and logs

      Staff paste customer details into a tool and logs keep them. Even with names removed, date of birth, postcode and transactions can still identify people.

    • Assuming one jurisdiction covers all

      A DIFC rule applied to an onshore bank, or Saudi transfer limits forgotten in a regional model. List every entity and country the system touches.

    1. Name the three layers and one example of each.

    Hard law: UAE or Saudi data protection law, DIFC Regulation 10. Supervisory expectation: CBUAE model management standards or SAMA frameworks. Principles: SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles.

    2. A DIFC bank builds a loan-pricing model. Which more likely binds it directly, the CBUAE note or DIFC Regulation 10?

    Regulation 10, if personal data is processed by an autonomous system. The CBUAE note is reported as aimed at onshore firms. The scope is unconfirmed, so say "likely" and let Legal confirm.

    3. Your vendor says its LLM service is "fully compliant". What do you ask?

    Compliant with what, where, under which contract? Where do prompts go, how long are they kept, and are they used for training?

    4. Why is "human in the loop" sometimes a weak control?

    Reviewers short of time, information or authority approve almost everything. Measure the override rate and time per case: near-zero overrides is a warning.

    5. A colleague says "SAMA requires banks to do X with AI". What do you do first?

    Ask for the document and clause. No dedicated SAMA AI instrument was confirmed, so the claim may come from general frameworks or a blog. Verify with Compliance.
