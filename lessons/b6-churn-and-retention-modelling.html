    B3.1 Day one About 10 minutes

    Churn, Propensity and Uplift

    Part 1

    What churn and uplift are

    In one sentenceChurn modelling finds customers who are likely to leave. Uplift modelling finds the ones where a phone call or offer actually changes the outcome. These are different groups.

    A bank customer almost never says goodbye. They fade out: the salary moves, card use drifts, the balance falls to a few hundred dirhams. The account closes months later. Retention is therefore two jobs: spot the fade early enough to act, and choose whom to call.

    Our running example: a bank has 1,000 customers and staff time for 100 calls. Who gets the calls?

    How the three models differ

    • Churn model: the chance a customer leaves if we do nothing. It is a smoke alarm: it senses heat, not whether the fire brigade can help.
    • Propensity model: the same machinery for any action, such as the chance a customer buys a savings product. Churn is propensity to leave.
    • Uplift model: the change in the chance of staying because we called. This is what a campaign needs.
    Part 2

    Four kinds of customer

    If we could see both futures for each customer (called, and not called), everyone would fall into one of four boxes.

    The top 100 by churn risk is mostly lost causes (already signed up elsewhere) and some persuadables. A churn model cannot tell them apart, because it never learned what happens when we act. Worse, calling a sleeping dog reminds them to compare fees, and they leave.

    Only persuadables repay the cost of a call.

    Part 3

    How to build it

    1. Define churn from behaviour. Current accounts have no end date. Common choices are account closure (certain but late), inactivity for a set number of days (easy, but it mislabels people who simply transact rarely), or salary stopped or balance down by over half (early and meaningful).
    2. Take a snapshot. Pick a date. Describe each customer using only what was known on or before it, then label them by what happens in the next 90 days or so. Anything computed after the date leaks the answer.
    3. Use changes as features. Transactions this quarter against last, balance trend, salary credits stopping, a new complaint. These beat static traits such as age.
    4. Set the horizon from your action lag. If a call takes 14 days to arrange, leavers inside those 14 days cannot be saved. Looking 7 days ahead is accurate but too late. Looking 180 days ahead is too faint. Choose in between.
    5. Run a randomised test for uplift. Call a random half and hold back the rest. Then compare: the simplest method fits one model on the called group, one on the held-back group, and subtracts them. Specialised uplift trees and causal forests do this more carefully.
    6. Call when uplift times customer value beats the call's cost. Never call negative uplift, however high the risk.

    In the Gulf, many retail customers are expatriates on employment visas. When the job ends the account goes dormant. That is real attrition but not retainable, so separate it out or the model wastes calls.

    Part 4

    How to read the results

    Uplift of +4 points
    A 70% chance of staying becomes 74% after a call. Of 100 calls, about 4 extra customers stay. Uplift of 0 means the call changes nothing. Negative means it backfires.
    Lift of 6 in the top 5%
    The top 5% of scored customers churn at 6 times the average rate. Lift of 1 means no better than picking at random.
    A score of 0.2
    If the model is calibrated, 20 in 100 such customers leave. You can multiply by the money at risk and add it up.
    Two customers, AED 400 and AED 400,000 balances
    They do not deserve equal attention. Rank by chance of leaving times value at risk.

    Uplift is never seen for one person, so judge the model with a Qini curve. Rank customers by predicted uplift, then plot the extra customers kept (called group minus held-back group) as you call more. A good model rises steeply, then flattens or falls. Effects are small and noisy, so ask for an interval from the bootstrap.

    With 100 calls, a churn ranking might keep a handful of extra customers. An uplift ranking, aimed at the persuadables, can keep several times more for the same cost.

    Part 5

    Which to use

    ModelAnswersNeedsWeak spot
    ChurnWho is likely to leave?History and a churn labelCannot say whom a call helps
    PropensityWho will take product X?Past take-upTargets people who would buy anyway
    UpliftWhom does a call change?A randomised holdoutSmall, noisy effects
    Next-best-actionWhich one offer per customer?Propensity or uplift per actionNever learns about actions it never shows

    Strong at:

    • Churn models: a gradient-boosted tree on snapshot features is the strong baseline. Logistic regression is the interpretable one.
    • Uplift models: spending a fixed call budget where it makes a difference, and avoiding sleeping dogs.

    Weak at:

    • Churn models: they explain the score, not the cause. "Salary credits stopped" is a signal you cannot fix. "Fee rose last month" may be a cause you can.
    • Uplift models: without a randomised holdout, "uplift" is just selection bias.

    Next-best-action (NBA) works like a waiter who suggests one dish per table. For each customer it removes anything ineligible or without marketing consent, scores each action by expected value net of cost, applies contact limits, and logs what happened. Randomise a small share of decisions so you keep learning.

    Show the decision rule

    Uplift = chance of staying if called − chance of staying if not called.

    Call when uplift × value of a kept customer > cost of the call. With uplift 0.04, value AED 6,000 and a call costing AED 30: 0.04 × 6,000 = AED 240, which beats AED 30, so call.

    In banking, contact rules apply: "do not call this person" is usually safe, but "call this person more" for a customer in difficulty needs policy sign-off.

    Part 6

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Calling the top of the churn list

      The most likely leavers include many lost causes. Ask who would respond, not who is at risk.

    • Calling a propensity model an uplift model

      If there was no randomised held-back group, nothing measured the effect of the call.

    • Using one inactivity window for everyone

      A heavy card user silent for 30 days is a warning. An occasional user is often silent that long while perfectly happy. Compare silence with the customer's own usual gap.

    • Picking the horizon to flatter the metric

      A 7-day prediction scores well but leaves no time to act. Pick the horizon from your action lag.

    1. In the 1,000-customer example, why is the top 100 by churn risk a poor call list?

    It is full of lost causes, who leave anyway, and may include sleeping dogs. The call only helps persuadables.

    2. What does an uplift of −3 points mean?

    The call makes the customer less likely to stay. Never contact them.

    3. Why must the uplift data come from a randomised test?

    Otherwise the called customers differ from the rest in hidden ways, and the measured effect is selection bias.

    4. A call costs AED 30, a kept customer is worth AED 6,000, uplift is 0.002. Call?

    0.002 × 6,000 = AED 12, which is below AED 30. Do not call.

    5. Why pick a 7-day horizon with care?

    It is accurate but the customer is already leaving, and acting takes time. Leavers inside the action lag cannot be saved.
