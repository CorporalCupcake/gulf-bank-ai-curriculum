    ISO 20022 and Open Finance

    Part 1

    What they are

    In one sentenceISO 20022 is the new standard format for payment messages, a form with labelled boxes instead of a free-text note. Open finance lets a customer share their accounts held at other firms with your bank.

    Our running example is one payment from a Dubai trading company to a supplier abroad. In the old format, a screening system must guess whether "AL FARSI TRDG LLC, DUBAI" matches a listed entity. In the new one, name, town and country are separate fields it can read directly.

    How it differs from what you know

    • Old SWIFT MT messages: a name and address squeezed into four 35-character lines, plus free text. ISO 20022 replaces them.
    • Open banking: shares payment accounts only. Open finance adds cards, loans, insurance and investments.
    • Your own data: shows what customers do with your products. Open finance shows what they hold elsewhere.

    Both invite the thought "more fields means more signal". That is only true if a field is filled often enough, filled honestly, and tells you something you did not already know.

    Part 2

    How it works

    ISO 20022: what is in the message

    It is a family of message types. The ones you will meet most:

    MessageWhat it isWhy it matters to a data scientist
    pain.001A customer tells their bank to payCorporate files often carry rich remittance data
    pacs.008 / pacs.009Bank-to-bank transfer, and cover transferThe core payment record. Cover payments now show the real payer and payee
    pacs.002 / pacs.004Payment status report, and payment returnRejection and return reasons work as labels and features

    The fields that pay off most:

    1. Structured parties and addresses. Separate town and country give exact country-risk features and better sanctions matching. SWIFT removes the unstructured address option for cross-border traffic in November 2026.
    2. Identifiers. A legal entity identifier (LEI) names a company, a BIC names a bank, and a UETR follows one payment across banks. They link the same entity without fuzzy name matching.
    3. Ultimate debtor and creditor. The real parties behind an intermediary. For anti-money-laundering (AML) work, often the most useful addition.
    4. Purpose codes. A coded reason in place of free text.

    Open finance: customer data, with consent

    The UAE Central Bank runs open finance under its Open Finance Regulation and the Al Tareq trust platform, phased in over time. Two rules shape how you use it.

    • Consent and purpose. The customer approves one use for a period, like a hotel key card that stops working at checkout. You cannot keep the data and train on it for ever.
    • Pull, not feed. Data arrives only when a customer grants access: rich for consenters, nothing for the rest.

    Main uses: credit decisions from cash flow (including customers with thin files), paper-free affordability checks, and mule detection. A mule account is one used to pass on stolen money, and one just funded from three other banks tells you something your own data cannot.

    Part 3

    How to read what it is worth

    Ask three questions of any new field or data source.

    • What is the fill rate? A field empty on 80% of payments is nearly useless. One filled with defaults ("N/A", "OTHR", the bank's own address) is worse, because a model treats it as information.
    • Who fills it in? Other banks' messages are only as good as their systems. Check quality per sending bank.
    • What does it add beyond what we have? Two forecasters reading the same radar add little to each other. A second signal that mostly repeats the first is nearly worthless, however strong it looks alone.

    Worked readings

    • Sanctions screening. A fuzzy match on "Mohammed Al Farsi" fires on dozens of listed people. An extra field (a different country, or an LEI resolving to another company) can clear some hits automatically. But only payments that carry the field benefit, so the saving is roughly fill rate × how much the field clears. If fields clear over half the false hits but only 60% of payments carry them, expect about a third, not a half.
    • Credit. The same open finance data may lift a model's accuracy (AUC) by 0.3 points for an established salaried customer, whose own data already shows most of it. For a new-to-bank customer it may add several points, because there is little else to go on. Value is the gain in your weakest segment, times the share of customers you can reach.
    Show the formula

    Realised saving ≈ fill rate × clearing rate. It says a field helps only on payments that carry it, so a headline figure measured on complete data overstates what you get while fill rate is low, as in the first months after migration.

    Part 4

    Which source to lean on

    SourceStrong atWeak at
    Parsed MT free textAvailable everywhere todayError-prone, guesswork
    ISO 20022 fieldsClean parties, links, purposesFill rate, defaults, version changes
    Your own transactionsFull coverage of your customersBlind to other banks
    Open finance dataNew information on thin-file customersConsenters only, strict purpose limits

    ISO 20022 is strong at replacing parsed text with fields and linking entities in a graph of who pays whom. It is weak at creating signal: richer data mostly makes existing signal cheaper to get.

    Open finance is strong at filling gaps where you know little. It is weak at reach, since only consenters appear, and consenters are self-selected.

    The safest habit is to measure on your own history rather than quote a vendor's "cuts false positives by 40%".

    Part 5

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Assuming a field is filled

      Most fields are optional. Profile fill rate and top values per field and per sender, and treat defaults as missing.

    • Training on the old format, scoring on the new

      A feature built from parsed text shifts once the fact arrives as a clean field. Compare distributions before and after cutover, and train on data that spans it.

    • Using data beyond the consent

      Data shared for an affordability check must not drift into marketing. Every dataset needs a link to a consent and a permitted purpose. If you cannot show it, ask compliance.

    • Training only on consenters

      They are not a random sample. Check calibration for consenters and non-consenters separately, and do not extrapolate.

    1. Name three ISO 20022 elements useful for AML and one limit on each.

    Structured address (senders vary), LEI (individuals have none), ultimate debtor or creditor (often empty), purpose code (often defaulted to "other"). The common limit is fill rate.

    2. Fields clear over half the false sanctions hits, but only 60% of payments carry them. Why is the real saving smaller?

    Only payments with the fields benefit, so the saving is roughly fill rate times clearing rate: about a third of the queue.

    3. Two signals separate good from bad customers equally well but are highly correlated. Is the second worth buying?

    Usually not. It mostly repeats the first, so it adds almost nothing beyond it.

    4. Why can open finance data add 0.3 points of AUC for one customer and 5 for another?

    The value is incremental. An established customer's own data already holds most of it. A new-to-bank customer's does not.

    5. An auto-clearing rule removes 60% of false hits. What else must you check?

    That it removes no true matches. Test on known true cases and sample the cleared hits, since one missed sanctions match costs far more than many wasted reviews.
