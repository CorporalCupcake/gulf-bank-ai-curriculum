    Arabic LLMs

    Part 1

    What an Arabic LLM is

    In one sentenceAn Arabic LLM is a large language model built or adapted to read and write Arabic well, so it copes with the Arabic customers really type, not only the formal Arabic of textbooks.

    Running example. A customer writes to a Gulf bank's WhatsApp: "I want to transfer money to my account but the app is stuck". They might write it in Arabic script, in Arabizi, or half in English. A colleague in Dubai understands all three instantly. A model often does not.

    Why: most models learn Arabic mainly from Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), the formal Arabic of news, books and government text. Customers write dialect, which has no standard spelling. It is like a student who passes the textbook exam and struggles with a street conversation.

    Part 2

    How it differs from other models

    • Versus a general frontier model (the big models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google): frontier models are strongest at reasoning and following long instructions, and their Arabic has improved a lot, but it is uneven on dialect. Arabic-specific models bring better Arabic text handling and local control, usually with less reasoning power.

    Where Arabic models come from

    They are all transformers, the same design as other LLMs. They differ in data, vocabulary and who controls them. There are three routes.

    • Train from scratch on a balanced Arabic and English mix. The first Jais models (2023) did this. It is expensive, but everything is Arabic-aware.
    • Adapt an existing model: take a strong open model, add Arabic and keep training. Cheaper, like retraining a good chef on a new cuisine. Later Jais and ALLaM models do this.
    • New design, then add Arabic: Falcon-H1 (from TII in Abu Dhabi) handles long inputs cheaply, and Falcon-Arabic models add Arabic on top.

    Part 3

    How the messy text is handled

    Why Arabic costs more: tokens

    A model reads tokens, small pieces of text from a fixed vocabulary. A vocabulary learned mostly from English is like a phrasebook for English speakers: English words get one entry, Arabic words are spelled out letter by letter.

    Arabic packs a lot into one word. The single word for "to my account" holds three parts, and without Arabic word pieces it breaks into meaningless fragments. The result:

    • Cost: pricing is per token, so the same message costs more.
    • Space: a fixed window holds fewer Arabic words.
    • Quality: the model has to relearn word structure.

    Recent frontier models have better multilingual vocabularies, so the gap is smaller than in 2023. Count tokens on your own text with the model's own tokeniser.

    Dialect, Arabizi and mixing

    • Gulf dialect (Khaleeji): "I want" is written as ابي, أبغى or ابا, not MSA's أريد. Spellings vary by speaker, so a keyword rule or an MSA-trained intent classifier misses requests.
    • Arabizi: Arabic typed in Latin letters by sound, with digits for sounds English lacks: 3 is ع, 7 is ح, 5 is خ. It looks like noise to a tokeniser and there is almost no training text in it.
    • Code-switching: English nouns carrying Arabic grammar, in one sentence. Models sometimes answer only one half.

    A practical pipeline

    1. Detect the form: Arabic script, Latin letters or mixed.
    2. Clean the script (for example, merge the several forms of the letter alef), but keep the original for the audit trail.
    3. Let the model read Arabizi directly: tell it the message is Gulf Arabic in Latin letters.
    4. Pull out amounts, dates and account numbers with code and validated patterns, not the model's reading.
    5. Classify or answer with a model chosen by your own tests.
    6. Send low-confidence cases to a person or a stronger model.
    Part 4

    How to read the results

    "Arabic" is four test sets, not one: MSA, Gulf dialect, Arabizi and mixed text. A single "Arabic accuracy" hides which one was measured.

    • 93% overall can hide a weak slice. If Arabizi is 15% of your traffic and the model scores 60% on it, the other 85% must score about 97% to reach 93%. Ask for results split by form.
    • Leaderboards shortlist, they do not decide. Public Arabic leaderboards are mostly MSA knowledge questions, often translated from English. None is a Gulf retail-banking test.
    • Small test sets cannot separate close models. 82% against 84% on 100 messages is within the noise. Build a set of a few hundred of your own labelled messages, split by form.

    Which to use

    • Choose a specialist when data cannot leave the bank, volume is high and the task narrow, or you must freeze a version for model-risk reasons.
    • Choose a frontier model when the task needs reasoning or tools.
    • Mixing is common: a small model sorts traffic, a frontier model handles the hard cases.

    In banking, ask four questions before choosing: which Arabic does our traffic use, what must the model do, may the data leave our servers, and how will we measure on our own sample.

    Part 6

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Choosing by leaderboard rank

      The top Arabic score measures mostly MSA questions, not your customers' messages. Test on 200 or more of your own.

    • One blended accuracy

      A high average can hide a very weak slice such as Arabizi. Ask for scores split by MSA, dialect, Arabizi and mixed, with counts.

    • Fluent but wrong

      Polished Arabic can misstate a local rule or a Shariah ruling, and non-experts will not notice. Have a qualified Arabic speaker grade a sample, and require an approved source for Islamic finance.

    • Trusting the model with numbers and names

      Arabic-Indic digits can be misread and a name swapped for a more common one. Extract identifiers with validated code and keep the raw text.

    1. Why can a leaderboard-topping Arabic model do badly on your WhatsApp channel?

    The leaderboard is mostly formal MSA questions. Your channel is dialect, Arabizi and mixed text: a different task. Only a test on your own messages answers it.

    2. Name two costs of a vocabulary that has seen little Arabic.

    More tokens per word, so higher cost and fewer words per window. Also worse modelling, since the pieces carry no meaning.

    3. Name three situations that favour a regional open model.

    Data must stay on your servers, high volume with a narrow task, and the need to freeze a version for model-risk control.

    4. Why extract amounts and account numbers with code?

    A model can misread digits or reorder them. Validated patterns are exact and checkable.
