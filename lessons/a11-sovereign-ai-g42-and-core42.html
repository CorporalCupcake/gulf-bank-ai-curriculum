    Sovereign AI and Confidential Computing

    Part 1

    What "sovereign" really means

    In one sentenceSovereign AI means running AI on infrastructure whose location, legal control and operators you can trust. Confidential computing is the hardware trick that keeps data hidden even from the machine's operator while the AI uses it.

    Think of a safe deposit box. You need three answers: is the vault in your country, whose law can force it open, and can the vault staff look inside while they move your box? Most people only ask the first.

    The classic mistake is "it is in the UAE, so it is sovereign". That only answers question 1.

    Why the Gulf cares

    AI needs data readable in GPU memory, on hardware the bank does not own. Gulf governments judged dependence on a few foreign data-centre owners a strategic risk, so they built national options. Names get mixed up in meetings, so here they are. Status may have moved since writing.

    • G42: an Abu Dhabi AI holding company, covering cloud, data centres and Arabic models such as Jais.
    • Core42: G42's sovereign cloud and GPU arm, an in-country alternative to a foreign cloud region.
    • Khazna: G42's data-centre builder.
    • Stargate UAE: a planned 1 GW AI cluster in Abu Dhabi, with OpenAI, Oracle, Nvidia and others. The first 200 MW is targeted for 2026. Announced gigawatts are plans, not capacity.
    • Humain: a Saudi AI company owned by the Public Investment Fund, launched in 2025. It is the Saudi national route for SAMA-regulated work.
    Part 2

    How it differs from ordinary cloud security

    Data can be protected in three states. The first two are routine; the third is the new one.

    • At rest: encrypted on disk.
    • In transit: encrypted on the network.
    • In use: the processor must hold data decrypted in memory to compute on it. A cloud administrator could read it there. This is the gap.

    The hardware that does this is a trusted execution environment (TEE). AI runs on GPUs, so the GPU needs it too (Nvidia added it from the H100). A CPU TEE with no GPU TEE leaves prompts in plaintext GPU memory.

    Confidential computing also differs from the privacy tools in the next lessons. It computes on plaintext inside protected hardware at small cost, so it is the first practical option for serving LLMs.

    Part 3

    How attestation works

    A TEE alone is only a claim. Remote attestation is the proof, like a tamper-evident seal you check before accepting a parcel.

    1. The bank asks the cloud host to start the model inside a TEE.
    2. The hardware signs a "measurement": a fingerprint of exactly what is running.
    3. The bank checks the signature and the fingerprint against what it expects.
    4. Only if they match does the bank release the decryption key.
    5. If the host was tampered with, the fingerprint differs and the key stays put.

    The bank trusts the chip vendor's signing, not the provider's word. If nothing checks the report before the key is released, the protection is decorative.

    Part 4

    How to think about it

    The rules behind it

    Three layers of rule shape what a bank can do. Your legal team owns the reading.

    • Data protection law: UAE Decree-Law 45 of 2021, the DIFC and ADGM regimes, and the Saudi PDPL. Transfers abroad need stated grounds.
    • Financial-sector rules: CBUAE and SAMA expectations on outsourcing, cloud and customer data, often stricter for core banking and payments.
    • National cyber and cloud frameworks: data is graded, and government and critical-infrastructure data is treated most tightly.

    These rules rarely say "use this company". They set conditions on location, transfer, control and audit. Foreign law matters too: a provider incorporated abroad can be ordered by its home courts to hand over data it controls, even if the data sits in your country. That is why customer-held keys matter.

    Own the GPUs or rent them?

    "The data must stay in the country, so buy our own GPUs" is usually wrong. Owning is like owning a car: it costs the same parked or driven. Renting is like taxis: you pay only for hours used, at a higher hourly price.

    • Break-even utilisation: the share of time a GPU must be busy before owning is cheaper. Above it, own. Below it, rent.
    • Bank workloads are bursty, so a cluster often sits at 15% busy and costs several times a rental.
    • The usual compromise: the sensitive core on bank-controlled or sovereign infrastructure with open-weight models, and external APIs only for non-sensitive data.

    Owning also needs power, cooling and a team that can run a GPU cluster, while renting may mean a queue.

    Which option to use

    Hosting optionResidencyForeign legal reachOperator can read in use?
    Public cloud, foreign regionNoYesYes
    Foreign cloud, in-country regionYesOften yesYes
    Sovereign cloud (e.g. Core42)YesDepends on contract and ownershipYes, unless TEE
    Sovereign cloud with TEE, customer keysYesReducedNo, if attested
    Bank's own data centreYesLowestOnly bank staff

    This is a teaching model, not legal advice. "Depends" is a judgement call, so check real rules with compliance and legal.

    • Strong at: confidential computing stops the host reading prompts, at small performance cost.
    • Weak at: it does not stop your own code leaking data, such as prompt injection. It does not remove trust in the chip maker, and side-channel attacks on these chips exist. It does not decide where data lives or which court can order disclosure.

    In banking, match each workload to the lightest option that meets its needs: public data on external APIs, customer data on sovereign or in-house infrastructure with a TEE.

    Part 6

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • "It is in the UAE, so it is sovereign"

      That is residency only. Ask who owns the platform, who holds the keys and which legal orders could reach the provider.

    • Confidential computing without attestation

      Find where the key is released. If nothing is verified first, the bank is trusting the provider's word again.

    • Protecting the CPU but not the GPU

      Prompts sit in plaintext GPU memory. Confirm both TEE modes are on and attested together.

    • Buying GPUs for a bursty workload

      Ask for expected utilisation and compare it with the break-even point.

    1. Name the three properties mixed under "sovereign". Which does a UAE data centre guarantee?

    Residency, jurisdiction and operator access. Only residency.

    2. What gap does confidential computing close?

    Data "in use": it stays encrypted in memory and is decrypted only inside the processor, so the host operator cannot read it.

    3. What does attestation add?

    Proof of what is running. The bank checks the signed measurement and releases the key only if it matches.

    4. A team wants to buy GPUs "because data must stay in the country". What do you ask?

    What utilisation to expect. A bursty workload is usually cheaper to rent in-country, since owning only wins above the break-even utilisation.

    5. Who is Humain, and who owns it?

    A Saudi AI company owned by the Public Investment Fund, launched in 2025.
