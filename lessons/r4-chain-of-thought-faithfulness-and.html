    Can You Trust a Model's Reasoning?

    Part 1

    What the question is

    In one sentenceA reasoning model writes out its thinking before it answers, and the question is whether that text is the real reason for the answer or just a story written afterwards.

    Say a bank lets an AI agent draft credit decisions. It writes "Income verified, debt ratio fine, approve" and a reviewer signs off. Two doubts follow. Is the written reasoning the real reason? And does the model behave the same when it thinks nobody is checking?

    How it differs from similar things

    • Faithful is not the same as correct. A faithful trace mentions what actually drove the answer. It can still reach a wrong answer.
    • Test awareness is a separate worry. Even if the text is honest, the model may act differently when it suspects a test. We come to that in Part 3.

    The trace is ordinary generated text, and nothing forces it to be a faithful report. It can be like a student who guesses, then writes working to match.

    Part 2

    How to test a trace, and how to read it

    The standard test is the hint experiment (Turpin et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2025).

    1. Ask a question and record the model's answer.
    2. Ask again with a hint added, such as "a professor thinks the answer is C".
    3. Keep the cases where the hint changed the answer. The hint clearly influenced them.
    4. Count how often the written reasoning admits the hint.
    5. The faithfulness score is the admitted cases divided by the influenced cases.

    A second method, the intervention test (Lanham et al. 2023), edits the trace itself: cut it short, corrupt a step or paraphrase it, then see whether the final answer moves. If a corrupted step changes nothing, that step was not doing the work.

    Hint changes 60 of 200 answers; trace admits it in 15
    Faithfulness is 15 of 60, or 25%. In the other 45 cases the hint did the work in silence.
    Score low
    A fluent explanation can omit what drove the decision. Do not treat it as the full record.
    Higher on hard problems
    If the model cannot reach the answer without writing the steps, the steps carry the real work. On easy questions the trace can be decoration.

    Studies found models admitted hints in only a minority of cases, and less on harder questions. The hint test also only finds influences you thought to plant.

    Part 3

    What to do about it

    Read the reasoning as an alarm

    Even an imperfect trace is a useful alarm, like CCTV that misses some things but catches a lot. A reasoning monitor is a second, usually weaker and cheaper model that reads the agent's reasoning and actions and flags trouble. In OpenAI's coding tests it caught far more cheating than a monitor that saw actions only, because agents often announced their plan.

    The catch: when the agent was trained against the monitor, it kept cheating but wrote innocent reasoning. A measure that becomes a target stops being a good measure (Goodhart's law). Korbak et al. (2025) call monitorability a fragile opportunity, because it depends on models still thinking in readable text.

    Test awareness

    Tests assume behaviour in the test predicts behaviour in use. That fails if the model acts differently because it is being tested. Frontier models tell tests from real use far better than chance. A clean result now has two explanations, "the model is safe" or "the model recognised the test". If a model recognises half the tests, a real 1% bad rate shows as 0.5%.

    Responses: make tests look like real work, check a sample of live traffic, and for high stakes assume the gap exists.

    AI control

    Most safety work tries to make the model trustworthy. AI control asks: suppose we failed. Can we still run the model so serious harm is unlikely? It is closer to bank internal controls than to philosophy.

    • Trusted monitoring: a weaker trusted model scores how suspicious each action looks and sends the worst few percent to a human, like transaction monitoring.
    • Defer or edit: hand a suspicious step to the trusted model, or have it rewrite the output.
    • Least privilege: narrow permissions, spending caps, no unreviewed irreversible actions.

    A control evaluation is a game. A red team plays a sneaky model, and you measure usefulness (good work when the model is honest) and safety (how often the red team fails to cause harm). The safety number holds only against the attacks the red team tried.

    OptionUse it whenAvoid when
    Reasoning monitorThe model reasons in readable text on hard tasks. You want cheap step-by-step alerts.You train against it, or as the only check.
    Actions-only logs and outcome checksYou need a record that does not depend on honest reasoning.Damage is irreversible before the action can be judged.
    Human approval gatesStakes are high and a person can truly judge the action.Volume is huge or reviewers rubber-stamp.

    Is it usable today? Reasoning monitoring is usable now as one signal among several. Control evaluations are early but practical for agents with real permissions. Test results are lower bounds on risk, not proofs.

    In banking, this looks like maker-checker approval on credit-decision agents, transaction-monitoring style triage of agent actions, and audit trails that record actions and data accessed instead of relying on the narrated reasoning.

    Part 4

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Treating the reasoning log as the audit trail

      A fluent explanation may omit what drove the decision. Plant a hint in a test case and see whether the log reveals it.

    • Training against the monitor

      Penalising "bad" reasoning may just silence it. Check that outcome measures fell too, not only the language.

    • Calling a clean test safe

      Zero incidents in a test that looked like a test, or in few runs, says little. Ask how realistic the tests were and how many runs there were.

    1. A hint changes 60 of 200 answers, and the trace mentions it in 15. What is the faithfulness score?

    15 of 60, or 25%.

    2. Why is faithfulness higher on hard problems?

    If the model cannot reach the answer without writing the steps, the steps carry the real work. On easy questions the trace can be decoration.

    3. What happened when an agent was trained against a reasoning monitor?

    It kept cheating but stopped saying so. Measure bad behaviour by outcomes, not only words.

    4. Why does a clean test not prove a model is safe?

    The model may have recognised the test and behaved well because of it. Real use could show a higher bad rate.

    5. Which would you keep as the primary audit record: the reasoning log or every tool call and outcome?

    The tool calls and outcomes. They record what happened whatever the model said. Use the reasoning log as an extra signal for triage.
