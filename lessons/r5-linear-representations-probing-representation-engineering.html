    Probing and Steering a Model From Inside

    Part 1

    What probing and steering are

    In one sentenceMany ideas inside a language model sit along a straight line of its internal numbers, so you can read an idea by measuring along that line (a probe) and write it by pushing along that line (steering), without retraining the model.

    Running example: a bank runs an open-weight model to sort customer messages, and we care about "this message is angry". At each layer the model holds an activation, a list of a few thousand numbers that acts as its memory for the current token.

    The linear representation hypothesis says that an idea like anger corresponds to a direction in that list of numbers. Calm messages sit at one end of the line and angry ones at the other.

    How it differs from other ways of changing a model

    • Fine-tuning. Changes the weights with training data. Probing and steering leave the weights alone.
    • SAEs (R3.3). Those discover many unknown directions with no labels. Here you already know the idea you want and look for its one direction.
    Part 2

    How it works

    Probing: reading the idea

    A probe is a small classifier trained on the model's frozen activations to predict a property of the input, like a thermometer placed inside it.

    1. Collect messages labelled calm or angry.
    2. Run them through the model and save the activations at one layer.
    3. Train a simple linear classifier (such as logistic regression) to tell the two groups apart. Its weights are the direction.
    4. Test on messages it has not seen, and compare with a control (see below).

    The simplest direction is the difference of the two group averages. Do not use the direction where the data vary most: that is usually something dull like message length or language, not anger.

    Steering: writing the idea

    Steering adds the direction to the activations while the model writes, which nudges it towards the idea.

    1. Gather contrast pairs: two inputs that differ only in the idea, such as an angry and a calm version of the same message.
    2. Average the difference of their activations. That is the steering vector.
    3. While the model generates, add the vector, scaled by a strength you choose, at a middle layer.
    4. Sweep strength and layer on held-out prompts, and check the text is still sensible.

    Part 3

    How to read it

    Probe accuracy 0.9
    The information is present in that layer. Nothing more: it does not show the model uses it.
    Real labels 90%, shuffled labels 50%
    A good sign. The gap between real and shuffled-label accuracy (the control) is what counts. A probe with more dimensions than examples can fit random labels perfectly.
    Steering strength too low or too high
    Too low does nothing. Too high pushes activations outside anything the model saw in training, and the text turns repetitive. Think of a radio dial: there is a sweet spot.

    Why a single line is only an approximation

    • Some ideas are not lines. Days of the week sit on a circle, so one direction cannot say "Tuesday".
    • Directions mix many neurons. Models pack in more ideas than neurons (R3.3).

    A related find: induction heads

    Given "Pay Al Noor Trading. Pay Al Ameen Exchange. Pay Al", a model picks a name seen earlier. An induction head does this: if the current token appeared before, predict what followed it last time. It needs two attention heads, one to note the previous token and one to match and copy. It copies rather than understands, so it can be wrong. Few-shot learning relies on more than this, and which mechanism matters most is not settled.

    Part 4

    Which to use, and when

    OptionPick it whenAvoid when
    Linear probeYou want to know if information is present, or to monitor a signal cheaplyYou need proof of use, or the idea is not a line
    Activation steeringYou have the weights and want a quick, reversible nudgeYou need guaranteed behaviour, or the model is closed
    PromptingThe behaviour can be described in words. Try this first.The behaviour keeps slipping
    Fine-tuningYou have labelled examples and need a lasting changeData is scarce
    Output classifierYou need a deterministic, auditable check outside the modelThe signal is only visible inside

    Strong at:

    • Cheap inspection. A probe needs no retraining, only activations you already compute.
    • Early warning. A probe can flag a harmful request before any output text exists.
    • Fast nudges. Steering takes minutes and adds no new weights.

    Weak at: API-only models (you need the activations), guarantees (a steering vector is statistical and anyone with the weights can remove it) and many behaviours at once.

    Is it usable today? Mostly research and model-risk work. Refusal in open chat models is mediated by a single direction, so behaviour can be inspected, and also edited out by anyone with the weights. Do not rely on steering as a control.

    In banking, a self-hosted assistant might carry a probe as an extra monitor, and a team could test whether a credit model relies on a proxy for a protected trait. Use prompting, fine-tuning and external guardrails for actual controls.

    Part 5

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Probe accuracy offered as proof the model knows something

      Decodable is not the same as used. Remove the direction and re-measure behaviour.

    • No control task

      With 4,096 inputs and 500 examples a probe can fit almost anything. Ask for shuffled-label results and held-out accuracy.

    • The probe finds a surface cue

      A fraud direction may fire on one word. Test on paraphrases and other languages.

    • Steering shown on a few prompts

      Tone may improve while accuracy or safety quietly drops. Test on held-out prompts with your normal evaluation suite, steering on and off.

    1. Why is the direction of largest variance usually a poor idea direction?

    Variance measures spread, not separation between the groups. The biggest axes are often dull things like length or language.

    2. A probe on 4,096 dimensions gets 99% on training and 51% on test, with 400 examples. What happened?

    Overfitting. With more dimensions than examples a probe can fit almost any labelling. Use held-out accuracy and a shuffled-label control.

    3. A probe predicts 'customer is a student' from an embedding at 90%. Does the model use student status?

    Not shown. It is decodable, perhaps through age or income. Remove the direction and see whether the model's scores change.

    4. You steer at strength 6 and replies turn into repetitive nonsense. Why?

    The push moved activations far outside what the model saw in training. There is a usable window of strength.

    5. You run open weights and must stop legal-sounding advice. Steering vector or output classifier?

    Start with the prompt and an output classifier: deterministic and auditable. A steering vector is a quick experiment, not a control.
