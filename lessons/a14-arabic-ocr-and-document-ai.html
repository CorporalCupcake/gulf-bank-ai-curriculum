    Arabic Documents and Islamic Finance

    Part 1

    What Arabic document AI is

    In one sentenceArabic document AI turns scanned or photographed Arabic paper into exact, checked fields (payer, amount, account number) that a bank system can trust.

    Running example. One line of a scanned transfer instruction: pay AED 125,000.00 to account AE07 0331 2345 6789 0123 456, in the name of a trading company. The reader sees one sentence. A machine sees stacked problems.

    • Arabic runs right to left, and letters change shape depending on their neighbours.
    • The amount and the IBAN (the international bank account number) run left to right inside the right-to-left line.
    • Digits may be 0-9 or Arabic-Indic (٠-٩), sometimes both on one page.

    Versus plain OCR: OCR only reads characters. Document AI also finds the fields and checks them.

    Part 2

    How it works

    1. Read the line. Arabic is cursive, so there are no clean gaps between letters. Modern readers take the whole line at once. Dots matter: ب ت ث ن differ only by dots, so a speck of noise changes the letter.
    2. Direction and digits. Text is stored in logical order but shown in visual order. Mix them up and your database holds reversed strings, which is a data bug, not a model bug. Convert digit systems early and keep the original.
    3. Layout and extraction: keep Arabic tables in right-to-left order, then pull out fields with rules or a model.
    4. Validate. Check formats, checksums and consistency between fields, then send failures to a person.

    Two ways to build it

    • Modular pipeline: separate stages you can inspect.
    • Vision-language model: give it the page image and ask for the fields. Simpler, but when the image is unclear it may write a plausible word instead of flagging doubt. Classic OCR makes visible garbage. A vision-language model makes fluent errors, which are harder to catch.
    Part 3

    How to read the accuracy

    Character error rate (CER) is the share of characters you would have to fix. 1% means one fix per 100 characters. It sounds small, but errors compound across a document.

    • A 23-character IBAN at 1% CER is fully right only about 79% of the time.
    • All four fields (name, date, amount, IBAN, about 60 characters) are right only about 55% of the time.
    • The business cares about exact fields, not characters. One wrong character in an IBAN pays the wrong beneficiary.

    So ask for the exact-match rate per field type on your own scans, split printed from handwritten, and look at the worst 5% of pages, because they fill your exception queue.

    • Checks turn silent errors into visible ones. An IBAN has a check digit, and the amount in figures should match the amount in words. Errors do not vanish, but most get caught.
    Part 4

    Why Arabic benchmarks mislead

    A public benchmark is like a driving-theory pass mark: it says little about driving in Dubai traffic.

    • Mostly formal MSA, while customers chat in dialect mixed with English.
    • Translated tests inherit the wrong world: a US tax-form question measures nothing you need.

    The remedy: build a test set of 200 to 1,000 anonymised examples from your own traffic, labelled by native speakers, split by MSA, dialect and mixed language. Use public benchmarks to shortlist and your own set to decide.

    Part 5

    AI for Islamic finance

    In one sentenceIslamic finance is banking built around bans on interest and excessive uncertainty, so AI here prepares evidence and a human Sharia authority decides.

    The ban on interest is called riba and the ban on excessive uncertainty is gharar. Instead, deals rest on real assets and shared risk. You will mainly meet two products:

    • Murabaha: the bank buys an asset and sells it to the customer at a disclosed mark-up, paid later.
    • Ijara: a lease, with ownership possibly passing at the end.

    Each Islamic bank has a Sharia supervisory board whose rulings (fatwas) bind it. Rules differ by country and change, so check current text.

    AI helps in two places, in rising order of risk:

    UseWhat the AI doesMain risk
    Equity screeningComputes ratios from company accounts and flags pass or fail. The board approves the method.Different methods give different answers; stale prices
    Contract checkingExtracts terms from a financing contract and compares them to a checklistA missed clause; confident but wrong reasoning on a new structure

    Equity screening and the moving base

    Screening asks whether a listed company is acceptable for a Sharia-compliant fund. It checks three things:

    • The business is not prohibited (alcohol, gambling, conventional banking).
    • Interest-bearing debt and deposits are below a limit relative to a base. 30% is commonly quoted.
    • Non-compliant income is a small share of total income, often about 5%.

    The base is the catch. Some index providers use 33%, and some divide by total assets instead of market capitalisation (the value of all shares). Confirm your board's method first. These figures are simplified, not a ruling.

    Worked reading. A company has market cap AED 10bn, total assets AED 12bn and debt AED 2.8bn. Debt over market cap is 28%, a pass. Then the share price falls 25%, so market cap becomes AED 7.5bn and the ratio becomes 2.8 / 7.5 = 37%, a fail. Debt over total assets is still 23%, a pass. The business has not changed, only the base.

    Teams therefore need a re-screening frequency and a tolerance band. They also need purification: giving the non-compliant share of a dividend to charity. If 3% of income is non-compliant, AED 6,000 of a AED 200,000 dividend goes to charity.

    Searching standards: split by clause, keep clause number and edition, and show the source text beside every summary.

    Part 7

    Common mistakes and quick check

    • Quoting character accuracy as the business case

      98.5% on characters can mean a minority of documents with every field right. Ask for exact-match rate per field on your own scans.

    • Trusting a vision-language model's clean output

      An unreadable name becomes a plausible one. Test with degraded images and cross-check with a checksum or second reader.

    • Screening on one method or a stale price

      A stock quietly crosses a limit after a market fall and stays in a compliant fund. Ask which method the board approved and how often re-screening runs.

    • Letting the model rule on compliance

      A summary shifts “must” to “may”, or a confident “this is compliant” appears with no scholar behind it. Without a named Sharia authority signing, nothing reaches a customer product.

    1. A system has a 0.5% character error rate. Why is that not enough to call it accurate?

    Errors compound across about 60 characters, and one wrong IBAN character pays the wrong person. Ask for exact-match rate per field.

    3. Why can a benchmark winner lose on your traffic?

    Benchmarks are mostly formal MSA or translated English. Your traffic is dialect and mixed text, so the ranking can flip.

    4. A company passes the debt screen, then its share price falls 25%. What happened?

    The base, market cap, shrank, so the debt ratio rose past the limit. The business did not change, which is why the board's method and re-screening frequency matter.

    5. Who decides whether a product is Sharia-compliant?

    The Sharia supervisory board. The AI finds, extracts and quotes, but never rules.
